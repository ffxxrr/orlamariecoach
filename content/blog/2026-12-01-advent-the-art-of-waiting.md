The sun goes down here in Donegal at about twenty past four now. By the time I've the kettle boiled after lunch, it feels like the day is already packing up.

Today is the first day of winter by the weather calendar, and Advent started on Sunday. Before it was a countdown to the presents, Advent was a season of waiting. People sat with the dark and waited for the light to come back.

On the Celtic Wheel, that's exactly what this stretch of the year is about. The days keep shortening until the winter solstice on Monday 21 December. At Newgrange, the old people built a whole passage tomb so the sunrise would reach right inside on those mornings. They knew the light was coming. They just had to wait for it.

## We're not great at waiting any more

Most of us hate a queue. We reach for the phone at a red light, and we're scrolling before the kettle has even started to rumble. December makes it worse. Life is lifing at the minute, and the list is as long as your arm.

But every wee wait in the day is a gap you could breathe in, if you want to.

## Try this: waiting well

Next time you're stuck waiting, in the queue at the shop, at the kettle, at the lights:

1. **Notice that you're waiting.** Notice the itch to fill the gap, too. That's grand, it's only habit.
2. **Leave the phone in your pocket** for this one wait, if you can.
3. **Feel your feet on the floor,** or your hands on the steering wheel.
4. **Take a few slow breaths,** a little longer on the way out than the way in.
5. **Look around you.** Find one thing you hadn't noticed: the light, a colour, someone's face.

Keep your eyes open the whole time. This is about being where you are, not drifting off.

If it only lasts four breaths, that's a minute of rest you didn't have before. Do it once a day through December and see how it feels by the solstice.

The light comes back on its own. We only have to wait well.

Mind yourself.

*Orla Marie*
