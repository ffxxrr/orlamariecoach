It's dark before five now, and the nights are long.

Long ago, nearly every townland in rural Ireland had one house where the neighbours gathered after dark. In Irish it was the *teach airneáin*, the night-visiting house. People called it the rambling house. You didn't knock. You came in, pulled a stool up to the fire, and somebody would call for a song or a story.

If you missed three nights running, people noticed. They'd be round to see were you all right.

## The soul friend

The old Irish had a word for the person who knows you that well: *anam chara*, the soul friend. Somebody you don't have to perform for. You can tell them life is lifing at the minute and they don't try to fix it, they just put the kettle on.

Most of us don't have a rambling house any more. We have group chats and busy diaries, and it's easy to go weeks without really talking to anybody. Winter can make that feel pretty lonely.

So this week I'd like to invite you to bring your soul friend to mind.

## Try this: a soul friend meditation

1. **Settle somewhere comfortable.** Close your eyes if that feels okay, or rest your gaze softly on the floor.
2. **Bring to mind someone who really sees you.** A friend, a sister, a neighbour, a grandparent, even someone who's no longer here. If nobody comes, that's all right too. You can picture the kind of friend you'd love to have.
3. **Notice how it feels in your body** to be with them. Maybe a warmth in the chest, or the shoulders dropping a wee bit.
4. **Stay with that feeling for a few breaths.**
5. **If you like, send them a message** to say you were thinking of them. Short and simple is grand.

You might be somebody's anam chara and not even know it. A wee text on a dark Tuesday evening could be the fire they pull their stool up to.

Mind yourself.

*Orla Marie*
