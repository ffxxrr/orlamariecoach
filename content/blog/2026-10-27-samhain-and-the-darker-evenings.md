The clocks went back on Sunday, and just like that, it's dark by teatime.

Most of us meet that with a groan. Lights on, curtains shut, telly up. I understand the pull. But the old Irish calendar saw this turn very differently.

## The dark half begins

On Saturday it's Samhain, the start of the Celtic new year. Our ancestors didn't see the dark half as something to get through. It was a time to slow down, gather in, and rest, the way the land does.

Samhain is also when the veil is said to be thinnest. In Donegal we're surrounded by thin places, the old stones and holy wells where the ordinary world feels a little closer to something bigger. Visiting them over the years has taught me that you don't need a famous site to feel that. A quiet five minutes at dusk will do it.

## Try this: the five-minute dusk sit

Some evening this week, before you switch the lights on:

1. **Find a window** and sit where you can see the sky.
2. **Let your breath settle.** No need to change it. Just notice it.
3. **Watch the light go.** Notice the colours fading, the shapes softening.
4. **Ask yourself one question:** what am I ready to let rest this winter?
5. **Then switch on one light,** slowly, on purpose.

That's it. Five minutes, and the dark becomes something you've welcomed rather than something that happened to you.

> The dark is not empty. It's where things grow before we can see them.

If this season tends to weigh on you, please be gentle with yourself. Go at your own pace, and if sitting with your thoughts feels like too much, keep your eyes open and your feet on the floor.

Mind yourself.

*Orla Marie*
