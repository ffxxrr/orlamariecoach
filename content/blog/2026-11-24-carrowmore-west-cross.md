There's a stone cross standing in a field outside Gleneely that might be 1,300 years old.

It's the Carrowmore West Cross, one of two high crosses on the site and one of six on Inishowen. It's short-armed and plain, no carvings at all, just weathered stone covered in pale lichen. It may date from as early as the 7th century, which makes it one of the earliest high crosses in Ireland. Its neighbour, the East Cross, is taller and carved with a figure of Christ with an angel on either side.

My husband and I share a love of places like this. We'll happily go down a wee back road to find a standing stone or an old cross in the corner of a field. Some of my favourite days have been spent this way, just the two of us.

## Thin places

The old Irish talk about thin places, where the space between this world and the next feels a bit thinner. I feel that at places like this. There's something about standing beside a stone that's been there for over a thousand years. The monastery here was founded in the 6th century, and people have been coming to this spot ever since.

Whatever is going on in my own head shrinks a bit next to that. The cross has stood through every kind of weather, and it's still here.

## Try this: standing still in an old place

You don't need a high cross. An old tree, a holy well, a graveyard wall or a stone on the shore all work.

1. **Find somewhere old** and stand or sit near it. Notice your feet on the ground.
2. **Look at it closely** for a minute. The colours, the moss and lichen, the way the weather has worn it.
3. **If it feels right, rest a hand on it.** Notice how it feels: cold, rough, damp, solid.
4. **Think of all the people** who have stood here before you, and let yourself be one more.
5. **Take three slow breaths** before you go.

If being still feels like too much, walk slowly around it instead, eyes open, taking it in from every side.

Mind yourself.

*Orla Marie*
