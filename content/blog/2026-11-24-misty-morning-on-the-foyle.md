The mist was sitting low on the Foyle the other morning when I headed out for a walk. I had a coffee from Patricia's in my hand and nowhere I needed to be for an hour.

Living between Derry and Donegal has its benefits. I can be in Inishowen one day and walking the river in Derry the next, and both have their own way of slowing me down.

That morning the water was so still the boats looked like they were standing on glass. The sun was trying its best to push through the cloud, and the far bank was only half there, soft and grey in the mist. The trees along the river have nearly let go of their leaves now. You can feel the year turning.

## Letting the season change

Autumn is the season of letting go. The trees don't fight it. They drop what they don't need and get ready to rest.

I stood by the railings with my hands round the warm cup and asked myself what I might let go of before winter. Not a big clear-out. Just one worry I'd been carrying around that wasn't really mine to carry.

## Try this: a mindful walk, coffee in hand

You don't need a river for this. A street, a park or the road outside your house will do.

1. **Bring something warm with you** if you like, a coffee or a tea. Notice the heat of the cup in your hands before your first sip.
2. **Walk a little slower than usual.** Feel each foot touch the ground.
3. **Look for three things that show the season changing.** Bare branches, wet leaves, your breath in the cold air, the light coming in lower.
4. **Take a sip and really taste it.** Let the walk pause while you do.
5. **Before you head home, ask yourself:** what's one thing I could let go of, the way the trees do?

If your mind wanders to the shopping list or the day ahead, that's grand. Notice where it went and come back to your feet, your cup, the air on your face.

Ten minutes is plenty. The mist will lift on its own, and so will a lot of what we carry.

Mind yourself.

*Orla Marie*
