Yesterday was All Souls Day.

In Ireland it has always been a day for visiting graves and saying the names of the people we've lost. In many homes a candle was left burning in the window, so the souls of the dead could find their way home.

This year, All Souls is close to my heart. Our family lost my beautiful nephew Shea, and we are still learning how to carry that. I'm not normally religious, but this year the comfort of tradition feels safe, and somewhere to land when everything can seem like it's out of control.

Grief isn't a problem to fix. It's how love feels when the person isn't here to receive it. A small ritual can give that love a place to rest.

## Lighting a candle, mindfully

Most of the time we light a candle without noticing. Done slowly, it becomes a meditation in itself.

Notice the match in your fingers. The scrape, the small flare, the smell of it. Watch the flame catch the wick, feel the warmth on your hand, then watch the light settle and steady.

In those few seconds there's nothing else to do. Your attention has somewhere to rest, and so does your heart. That's all mindfulness really is: being fully here for one small thing.

## Try this: a candle for the ones we miss

Some evening this week, when the house is quiet:

1. **Light a candle slowly,** noticing each part of it, and set it somewhere you can see it.
2. **Say their name,** out loud or quietly to yourself.
3. **Breathe in slowly,** and bring to mind one thing they gave you: a laugh, a habit, a way of seeing things.
4. **Breathe out,** and let yourself feel whatever is there. There's no right way to do this.
5. **Stay as long as feels right,** then let the candle burn a while longer.

If grief feels too heavy to sit with right now, keep your eyes open, feel your feet on the floor, and come back to it another day. Be as gentle with yourself as you would be with a friend.

Tonight I'll light a candle for Shea and his mammy. If you're lighting one too, know you're not on your own.

Mind yourself.

*Orla Marie*
